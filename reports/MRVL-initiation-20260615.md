# MRVL: Marvell Technology, Inc. — Initiation

## Thesis

Marvell Technology is a fabless semiconductor designer that has pivoted from a diversified communications chip vendor into an AI infrastructure pure-play, with 74% of revenue now coming from data center customers [1]. The stock matters right now because management just raised FY2027 revenue guidance to ~$11.5B (+40% YoY) and FY2028 to ~$16.5B (+45% YoY), driven by an interconnect business now expected to grow 70%+ this year and custom silicon that management says will more than double in FY2028 [2]. The one number to watch from the Q2 FY27 print (late August 2026) is data center revenue: bulls want $2.1B+ (implying ~50% YoY growth), while bears will flag anything below $2.0B as evidence the interconnect ramp is decelerating.

## Business snapshot

Marvell designs high-speed interconnects (optical DSPs, TIAs, drivers), Ethernet switches, custom ASICs, and storage controllers for hyperscale data centers and telecom carriers. It sells to OEMs and ODMs, with two customers (one distributor, one direct) representing 51% of FY2026 revenue and the top ten customers representing 82% [1]. The company is fabless, outsourcing manufacturing primarily to Taiwan-based foundries.

### Three debates

#### Debate 1: Is the $10B+ custom silicon target by FY2029 achievable, or is it priced in?
- **Bulls argue:** Marvell has custom engagements with all five U.S. hyperscalers, and CEO Matt Murphy stated on the Q1 FY27 call that "every sort of program we looked at a year ago is larger when we look a year later," with a new tier-1 XPU program entering high-volume manufacturing in FY2028 [2].
- **Bears argue:** Custom silicon is concentrated among a small number of customers, and Broadcom already dominates this space with a decade-long head start; Marvell's $10B target implies taking significant share from an entrenched competitor [4].
- **What resolves it:** FY2028 custom silicon revenue disclosure — bulls want >$3B, bears flag <$2.5B.

#### Debate 2: Can interconnect growth sustain 70%+ as cloud CapEx moderates?
- **Bulls argue:** Management raised interconnect growth guidance from 50% to 70%+ for FY2027 because 1.6T PAM4 DSPs and CoherentLight products are seeing demand acceleration, and the NVIDIA partnership expands the TAM [2].
- **Bears argue:** Cloud CapEx growth is expected to moderate into the 30% range, and Marvell's interconnect business has historically been lumpier; the 70% guide could be front-loaded [2].
- **What resolves it:** Q2 and Q3 FY27 data center revenue trajectory — bulls want sequential growth >10%, bears flag <8%.

#### Debate 3: Is customer concentration a fatal flaw?
- **Bulls argue:** Distributor A (37% of FY2026 revenue) and Direct Customer A (14%) are diversified end-customer bases, and the hyperscaler relationships are deepening across multiple product lines [1].
- **Bears argue:** The top two customers represent 51% of revenue, and any loss of share at a major hyperscaler would be catastrophic; the 10-K explicitly warns that "the loss of any of our large customers...would likely harm our financial condition" [1].
- **What resolves it:** Customer concentration disclosure in the FY2026 10-K — any increase in the top-two customer share above 55% would amplify bear concerns.

## Bull / Bear / Base

### Bull
75x FY2028E EPS of $6.17 = $463 / +65% from current. This case requires Marvell to hit the high end of its FY2028 revenue guidance, sustain custom silicon momentum into FY2029, and expand operating margins toward the 38-40% target as opex grows in the mid-to-high teens while revenue compounds at 45% [2].

### Bear
45x FY2027E EPS of $3.44 (15% below consensus) = $155 / -45% from current. This case materializes if cloud CapEx slows faster than expected, a major custom silicon customer delays or cancels a program, or Broadcom wins back share in the ASIC market. The stock's 97x LTM P/E and 69x forward P/E leave little room for execution error.

### Base
69x FY2027E EPS of $4.05 held flat = $281 / +0% from current. This is the continuation-of-current-trajectory case where Marvell executes on its FY2027 guidance but the multiple compresses as growth naturally decelerates into FY2028. The stock is priced for perfection in the near term, and the base case implies returns come from EPS compounding rather than multiple expansion.

## Top 3 risks

- **Customer concentration:** The top two customers represented 51% of FY2026 revenue, and any loss of share or inventory digestion at a major hyperscaler would disproportionately impact results [1].
- **Taiwan/China supply chain and export controls:** Most manufacturing is outsourced to Taiwan-based foundries, and U.S. export restrictions on Chinese customers have already reduced demand; further escalation would hurt [1].
- **AI infrastructure spending cyclicality:** The 10-K explicitly warns that "the current level of capital expenditure on AI infrastructure may not be sustainable," and a significant reduction would harm Marvell's data center revenue, which now represents 74% of the total [1].

### Competitive Landscape

Marvell describes its competitive position as dependent on "the performance, features, quality and price of our products," the timing of new product introductions, and its ability to secure foundry capacity [1]. The company competes with Broadcom in custom ASICs, with NVIDIA in AI infrastructure (though increasingly as a partner), and with a range of players in interconnects and switching.

Broadcom's decade-long hyperscaler relationships and $10B+ AI revenue scale create a high bar for Marvell to clear.
