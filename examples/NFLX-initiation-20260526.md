## Business Overview

Netflix, Inc. is one of the world's leading entertainment services, offering TV series, films, games, and live programming across a wide variety of genres and languages. Members can play, pause, and resume watching as much as they want, anytime, anywhere [1].

**Revenue Model:** Netflix primarily derives revenues from monthly membership fees for streaming content. The company offers a variety of streaming membership plans with prices varying by country and plan features. As of December 31, 2025, pricing on paid plans ranged from the U.S. dollar equivalent of $1 to $37 per month [1]. The company also earns revenue from advertisements on its ad-supported subscription plan, consumer products, live experiences, and various other sources. Non-subscription revenue was not a material component in FY2025 [1].

**Key Operating Metrics:** Management discontinued reporting membership numbers in 2025, focusing instead on revenue and operating margin as primary financial metrics [1]. The company ended 2025 with more than 325 million paid members [2]. Management tracks "member quality" metrics that correlate with retention, and reported achieving an all-time high in this primary quality metric in both Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 [3].

**Strategic Priorities (2026):**
1. Deliver more entertainment value through series, films, games, live programming, and podcasts
2. Leverage technology to improve content delivery, discovery, and creation (including GenAI)
3. Improve monetization through pricing, advertising, and distribution [3]

### Quality-of-Earnings Notes

- **WBD termination fee boosted Q1 2026 EPS:** The reported Q1 2026 EPS of $1.23 included a $2.8 billion termination fee from the abandoned Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition, which flowed through "interest and other income." Excluding this one-time item, Q1 2026 EPS would have been approximately $0.56, below consensus of $0.76 [4].
- **SBC-adjusted FCF is 87% of reported FCF:** Stock-based compensation of ~$1.47B annually represents a modest 3.1% of revenue, indicating limited economic dilution.
- **Content amortization growing slower than revenue:** Content amortization grew ~11% in FY2025 vs. 16% revenue growth, contributing to margin expansion.

## Management Commentary

**Q1 2026 Earnings Themes:**
- Management maintained full-year 2026 guidance: revenue growth of 12-14% and operating margin of 31.5% [3]
- Advertising business on track to roughly double to ~$3 billion in 2026 [3]
- Q2 2026 guidance disappointed the market, with revenue guided to ~$12.5B (below some expectations) and operating income of $4.11B vs. consensus $4.34B [5]
- Content amortization expected to grow ~10% in 2026, with the highest YoY growth rate in Q2 before decelerating [5]
- Reed Hastings announced he will not stand for reelection as board chair, marking the completion of a long-planned succession process [3]

**Strategic Commentary:**
- The WBD deal was characterized as a "nice-to-have, not a need-to-have." Management emphasized investment discipline in walking away when costs exceeded value [3]
- APAC was the strongest FX-neutral revenue growth region in Q1, driven by Japan (World Baseball Classic), India, Korea, and Southeast Asia [3]
- Live events strategy remains focused on "big breakthrough events" rather than regular season packages [3]
- Podcasting and gaming represent long-term optionality but remain small relative to total content spend [3]

## Market Opportunity

**Netflix's TAM Claim:** Management estimates its addressable market at $670 billion as of 2026, representing consumer and advertising spend in countries and categories where Netflix directly participates. The company estimates it has captured only ~7% of this addressable revenue and ~5% of global TV viewing time [3].

**Independent TAM Estimates:**
- Research and Markets estimates the global media streaming market at $158 billion in 2026, growing to $416.8 billion by 2030 (Grand View Research) [6]
- Ampere Analysis forecasts global entertainment content spending of $255 billion in 2026, with streaming platforms spending $101 billion [7]

**Implied Market Share:**
- Against Netflix's claimed $670B TAM: ~6.7% (FY2025 revenue)
- Against independent $158B streaming TAM (2026): ~28.6%

**TAM Methodology Note:** The wide divergence reflects different scopes—Netflix's TAM includes all consumer entertainment and advertising spend in its markets, while independent estimates focus narrowly on subscription and ad-supported streaming revenue. Netflix's broader definition is appropriate for a company competing for leisure time across all entertainment formats.

## Key Risks

From 10-K Item 1A and recent earnings commentary [1][3]:

1. **Member Growth Deceleration:** In mature markets (UCAN), penetration is high and growth is slower. The company must continually add new members to replace cancellations and grow.
2. **Content Cost Inflation:** Content costs are largely fixed. If growth slows, Netflix may not be able to adjust expenditures commensurately, impacting margins.
3. **Competition Intensification:** Competitors include streaming services, linear TV, gaming, social media, and piracy. Several competitors have significant financial resources.
4. **Advertising Execution Risk:** The ad-supported plan is relatively new. ARM (average revenue per membership) for ad-tier remains below ad-free plans.
5. **Regulatory Risk:** Growing regulatory action globally, including content quotas, investment obligations, and potential restrictions on ownership rights.
6. **WBD Deal Aftermath:** While the deal was abandoned, the attempt signals appetite for large M&A. Future deals could strain the balance sheet or distract management.
7. **Key Person Risk:** Reed Hastings' departure as board chair marks the end of an era. Co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters have been in place since 2023, but governance transition adds uncertainty.
8. **Foreign Exchange Risk:** ~56% of revenue comes from outside UCAN, creating FX headwinds.

## Investment Framework

### Bull / Bear / Base Cases

**Bull Case:** Netflix sustains 15%+ revenue growth through a combination of membership growth (especially in APAC), price increases, and advertising scaling to $3B+ annually. Operating margins expand toward 33-35% as content amortization grows slower than revenue. The stock re-rates from 24x forward earnings toward 30x (peer SPOT trades at 34x forward), implying 25-35% upside from current levels. The bull case is confirmed if Q2 2026 revenue exceeds $12.6B and operating margin holds above 31%.

**Bear Case:** Q2 guidance miss signals decelerating growth. Revenue growth slows to single digits as mature markets saturate and competition intensifies. Content costs rise faster than expected, compressing margins. The stock's multiple compresses toward 20x forward earnings (traditional media peer level), implying 15-20% downside. The bear case materializes if FY2026 revenue comes in below $50B or operating margin misses the 31.5% target.

**Base Case:** Netflix delivers on FY2026 guidance (12-14% revenue growth, 31.5% operating margin). The stock trades sideways to modestly higher as earnings growth drives the return. At 24-25x forward earnings on $3.60 EPS, the stock implies a 10-15% annual return from earnings growth alone, with limited multiple expansion.

### Key Debates

**1. Is the WBD deal failure a positive or negative?**
- *Bull:* Demonstrates investment discipline. Netflix walked away when costs exceeded value, preserving balance sheet strength and management focus. The $2.8B termination fee boosted near-term cash.
- *Bear:* Suggests Netflix sees organic growth slowing enough to require large M&A. The deal would have added significant content IP and studio capabilities.
- *Resolution:* Q2-Q3 2026 earnings will show whether Netflix can sustain growth without WBD's assets.

**2. Is the advertising business material enough to matter?**
- *Bull:* Ad revenue doubling to $3B in 2026 represents a new high-margin revenue stream. Programmatic buying is scaling, and the advertiser base grew 70% in 2025 to 4,000+.
- *Bear:* At $3B, ads are only ~6% of total revenue. The ad-tier ARM remains below ad-free, creating a drag on average monetization.
- *Resolution:* FY2026 ad revenue results and ARM trends will clarify the trajectory.

**3. Is the stock's 34% decline from highs justified?**
- *Bull:* Fundamentals remain strong—16% revenue growth, 29.5% operating margins, $11.9B LTM FCF. The decline reflects sentiment, not deterioration.
- *Bear:* The Q2 guidance miss, Hastings' departure, and WBD deal failure represent real risks that the market is appropriately pricing in.
- *Resolution:* Q2 2026 earnings (July 16) will test whether guidance was conservative or whether deceleration is real.
