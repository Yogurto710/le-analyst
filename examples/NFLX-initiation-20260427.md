## Business Overview

Netflix, Inc. is one of the world's leading entertainment services offering TV series, films, games and live programming across a wide variety of genres and languages. Members can play, pause and resume watching as much as they want, anytime, anywhere, and can change their plans at any time [1]. The company operates as a single operating segment, with revenues primarily derived from monthly membership fees for streaming content. Netflix offers a variety of streaming membership plans with pricing varying by country and plan features, ranging from the U.S. dollar equivalent of $1 to $37 per month as of December 31, 2025 [1].

The company's core strategy is to grow globally within the parameters of its operating margin target. Netflix strives to continuously improve members' experience by offering compelling content, a range of pricing plans (including an ad-supported subscription plan), and an enhanced user interface. Management tracks revenue and operating margin as the primary financial metrics representing business performance, having discontinued reporting membership numbers in 2025 [1].

Revenue by region for 2025 was: UCAN ($20.0B, 44% of total), EMEA ($14.5B, 32%), LATAM ($5.4B, 12%), and APAC ($5.4B, 12%). APAC was the fastest-growing region at 21% reported (22% constant currency) [1].

## Financial Profile

Netflix delivered strong results in 2025, with revenue growing 16% to $45.2B and operating income expanding 28% to $13.3B, driving operating margin up 280bps to 29.5%. The company generated $10.1B in operating cash flow and repurchased $9.1B of stock. Net income grew 26% to $11.0B [1].

### Quality-of-Earnings Notes

SBC-adjusted FCF is 96.9% of reported FCF, indicating minimal SBC dilution. Netflix's Q1 2026 net income of $5.3B included approximately $2.8B from the WBD deal termination fee, a non-recurring item that materially inflated GAAP earnings above operating performance [2]. Operating income of $4.0B in Q1 2026 is the cleaner metric, representing a 32.3% operating margin.

## Management Commentary

Management maintained full-year 2026 guidance for revenue growth of 12%-14% and operating margin of 31.5% [2]. The company reiterated its goal of roughly doubling the advertising business to about $3 billion in 2026. Co-CEO Gregory Peters highlighted that Netflix has captured about 7% of addressable revenue in countries and categories it directly participates in, which management estimates at $670 billion [2].

Key strategic priorities outlined on the Q1 2026 call include: (1) delivering more entertainment value through series, films, live sports, podcasts, and games; (2) leveraging technology to improve the service, including GenAI for content production and personalization; and (3) improving monetization through pricing, partnerships, and advertising [2].

The terminated Warner Bros. Discovery transaction was described as a "nice-to-have, not a need-to-have." Management emphasized investment discipline in walking away when the deal's cost exceeded net value. The $275M M&A-related expense guidance remains intact, with no material impact on operating margin outlook [2].

Reed Hastings announced he will not stand for reelection as board chair at the June 2026 annual meeting, marking the end of an era but with no change in strategic direction [2].

## Market Opportunity

Netflix estimates its total addressable market at approximately $670 billion as of 2026, representing addressable revenue in countries and categories where it currently participates. Management notes it has captured only 7% of this TAM and accounts for just 5% of global TV view share [2].

An independent estimate from Ampere Analysis puts global streaming subscription revenue at $157.1 billion in 2025 (up 14% YoY), with the market on track to exceed $200 billion by 2030 [3]. This figure represents subscription revenue only and excludes advertising, games, and other ancillary revenue streams.

The divergence reflects scope differences: Netflix's TAM includes all entertainment spending (theatrical, gaming, live events, merchandise), while Ampere focuses narrowly on streaming subscriptions. TAM methodology limitations include varying definitions of "streaming," exclusion of piracy, and rapidly shifting consumer behavior across markets.

Netflix emphasizes that consumers maintain simultaneous relationships with multiple entertainment sources.

## Key Risks

From the 10-K Item 1A and recent developments [1]:

1. **Member Acquisition and Retention:** Netflix must continually add members to replace cancellations and grow. Growth rates fluctuate across jurisdictions, with slower growth in mature markets. Competitors' offerings and pricing may adversely impact retention.

2. **Content Cost and Quality:** Content costs are largely fixed. If growth slows, the company may not be able to adjust expenditures commensurately. Original programming requires significant upfront cash. Content providers may refuse to license on acceptable terms.

3. **Advertising Business Uncertainty:** Netflix has limited experience in advertising. Success depends on attracting advertisers, competing for ad spend, measurement accuracy, and regulatory developments around privacy and targeting.

4. **WBD Transaction Overhang:** While the deal was terminated, the $42.2B bridge facility commitment remains outstanding until January 2026 amendments are fully unwound. The episode demonstrated Netflix's willingness to pursue transformative M&A, creating uncertainty about future capital allocation.

5. **Regulatory and Tax Risks:** Growing regulatory action globally, including cultural support legislation, content quotas, and network usage fees. Brazil non-income tax assessments totaled ~$700M in deposits.

6. **Competition and Piracy:** Intense competition from streaming providers, gaming, social media, and linear TV. Piracy offers "virtually all content for free" and is subject to rapid global growth.

7. **Reed Hastings Departure:** The founder's exit as board chair in June 2026 marks a governance transition, though management emphasizes continuity.

## Investment Framework

### Bull / Bear / Base Cases

**Bull Case:** Netflix continues to execute on its three-pillar strategy—more entertainment value, technology leverage, and improved monetization. Revenue growth sustains at 14%+ (above guidance midpoint) driven by APAC expansion, advertising scaling to $3B+, and successful price increases with minimal churn. Operating margin expands beyond 31.5% as content amortization growth moderates. The stock re-rates from 7.7x forward EV/Revenue toward 9.0x-9.5x (closer to historical averages), implying 20-30% upside. Confirmation trigger: Q2 2026 revenue growth >14% and operating margin >32%.

**Bear Case:** Growth decelerates faster than expected as mature markets saturate and competition intensifies. The advertising business fails to scale profitably, with CPM pressure and measurement challenges. Content costs rise faster than revenue, compressing margins. The WBD deal termination signals M&A discipline but also reveals limits to inorganic growth. If revenue growth drops to single digits, the multiple could compress toward 6.0x-6.5x forward EV/Revenue (peer floor), implying 15-25% downside. Confirmation trigger: Q2 2026 revenue growth <11% or operating margin <31%.

**Base Case:** Netflix delivers on its 12-14% revenue growth guidance and 31.5% operating margin target. The advertising business grows but remains a small portion of revenue. The stock trades sideways with modest multiple compression as growth decelerates, generating returns primarily through buybacks (8.0B remaining authorization). The market is pricing in a continuation of current trends at ~7.7x forward EV/Revenue, which is reasonable if execution remains steady.

### Key Debates

**1. Is the advertising business a material value driver or a distraction?**
Netflix targets $3B in ad revenue for 2026 (roughly doubling from ~$1.5B in 2025), which would still be only ~6% of total revenue. Bulls argue that programmatic ad buying (approaching 50% of non-live ads) and a 70%+ YoY advertiser base expansion to 4,000+ advertisers signal a scalable high-margin business [2]. Bears counter that Netflix's ad load remains light, CPMs are unproven at scale, and the $3B target is immaterial to a $51B revenue base. This debate is resolved by Q4 2026 ad revenue disclosure and 2027 guidance.

**2. Did Netflix's WBD pursuit reveal strategic desperation or disciplined optionality?**
Bulls view the $82.7B WBD pursuit and subsequent walk-away as evidence of disciplined capital allocation—management tested its M&A muscle and walked when value was destroyed [2]. Bears worry that the pursuit itself reveals concern about organic growth saturation, and the termination fee ($2.8B) plus financing costs represent real value destruction. This debate is resolved by 2026 organic growth performance and whether Netflix pursues another large acquisition.

**3. Is the stock's 31% decline from highs a buying opportunity or a value trap?**
Bulls point to 16% revenue growth, 29.5% operating margins, and $11.9B FCF as evidence of a high-quality business trading at a discount to history (8.7x vs. 9.9x average EV/Revenue). Bears note that forward growth is guided to 12-14%, the Q2 2026 margin guide disappointed, and the stock still trades at 32.7x P/FCF with limited near-term catalysts. This debate is resolved by Q2 2026 earnings on July 16 and full-year guidance updates.

## Open Questions

1. **Netflix 2025 annual stock-based compensation:** Finbox reports $368.4M, but this could not be independently verified from the 10-K notes. The 10-K mentions SBC is included in cost of revenues, technology and development, and G&A, but an explicit total SBC figure was not located in the extracted Item 7. Analysts should verify from Note 10 (Stockholders' Equity) in the full 10-K.

2. **WBD streaming revenue and EBITDA:** WBD reports consolidated results but does not break out pure "streaming" revenue separate from linear networks. The $10.9B streaming revenue figure is an approximation based on segment reporting. Streaming Adjusted EBITDA of ~$5.3B is an estimate.

3. **Disney DTC streaming-only metrics:** Disney reports DTC revenue including Disney+, Hulu, and ESPN+, but the exact streaming-only revenue figure is not cleanly separable from the $94.4B total company revenue. Streaming operating income of $1.3B for FY2025 is reported.

4. **YouTube as peer comparability:** YouTube is a division of Alphabet and lacks standalone financials. The $60B+ revenue figure includes ads and subscriptions but cannot be directly compared to Netflix's EV/Revenue.

5. **Netflix gross profit 2024:** The 10-K Item 7 did not explicitly state gross profit; the $17,963M figure is from Macrotrends and should be verified against the full 10-K income statement.

6. **Q1 2026 FCF:** The $5.1B FCF figure cited in some sources includes the $2.8B WBD termination fee. Clean operating FCF is lower and should be calculated as operating CF ($5,290M) minus CapEx ($196M) = ~$5.1B, but the termination fee is in operating activities. Analysts should adjust.
